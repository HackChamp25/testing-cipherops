About Cipherops

Post-quantum DNSSEC, measured on a real hierarchy.

Cipherops is a hands-on lab that signs a root, TLD and leaf DNSSEC hierarchy, then lets you watch, measure and compare how Merkle Tree Ladder (MTL) signatures behave against classical DNSSEC and plain SLH-DSA.

Our goal is to give the future quantum industry a practical, data-driven path to secure DNSSEC, grounded in how real-time DNS operations truly work. The Cipherops team

Why it exists

Post-quantum signatures are large, and DNS answers have to stay small. MTL signs a zone once and gives each record a short proof. A paper can claim that trade-off; this lab lets you see sign time, packet size and cold versus warm lookups on real servers.

Who it is for

Researchers and engineers evaluating post-quantum DNSSEC, DNS operators weighing the cost of larger signatures, and reviewers who want to check the numbers on a running system.

What you can do

Prepare and replaySign the hierarchy, watch each zone finish live, then replay how MTL builds its tree and ladder.
Live lookupsSend a real query through a validating resolver and read the packets it causes.
BenchmarkRun the PS-036 benchmark across dataset sizes, with repeated trials.
ComparePut MTL beside SLH-DSA, ECDSA and RSA: signing, size, bytes on the wire and cache.

How it works

Set upPick a zone size and the scenarios to sign, build the images, then Prepare.
ExploreStart the lab, send lookups and read the capture, cold and warm.
MeasureRun the benchmark, then compare the algorithms in one place.

Built to be trusted

Open source · MITRuns on your machine Unmodified Verisign MTLPinned builds

Signed zones and captures stay on your computer. The dashboard has no accounts and does not upload lab data. Signing uses the Verisign MTL reference stack as published, with pinned OpenSSL, liboqs, NSD and Unbound versions.
